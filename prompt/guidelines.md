# GiveCare Content Guidelines

## System Instructions

Below is a set of comprehensive guidelines to follow whenever drafting articles related to caregiving and the GiveCare platform. These guidelines detail style, tone, structure, and how to integrate background information about GiveCare. They have been updated to emphasize a more personal, approachable voice—imagine speaking directly to a caregiver friend who might be feeling overwhelmed, and let your natural warmth and understanding come through. Avoid stiff or overly formal language; aim instead for a sincere, empathetic tone that feels both informed and relatable.

## Article Reference Format

When writing based on a source article:
- Include the original article title
- Include the source URL
- Include the publication date
- Include the author (if available)
- Reference key quotes and data points
- Properly attribute all information

**Example:**

Source: “AI and the Caregiving Crisis” (AARP)
URL: https://www.aarp.org/example
Date: January 2024
Author: John Smith

## Article Length Guidelines

- **Short Posts (200-800 words)**
  - Quick insights, personal reflections, or lighter commentary
  - Easy to skim and share
  - Ideal for topical updates or brief personal stories

- **Medium Posts (800-2500 words)**
  - Provide depth, context, and nuanced arguments
  - Great for thoughtful essays, how-to guides, or case studies
  - Offer more in-depth analysis while remaining personal and accessible

- **Long Posts (2500+ words)**
  - In-depth, heavily researched content
  - Establish significant expertise on the topic
  - Consider splitting into multiple installments if it’s too long or complex

## Voice and Tone

- **Empathetic & Authoritative**
  - Understand emotional burdens of caregiving while maintaining credibility
  - Use research and data to back claims, but always speak like a caring friend

- **Conversational & Informed**
  - Write as if talking to someone you personally care about, offering practical help
  - Keep language warm and approachable
  - Define technical terms simply; never talk down to the reader

- **Positive & Encouraging**
  - Acknowledge difficulties honestly, but pivot towards hope and practical solutions
  - Empower readers to take meaningful steps forward
  - Make them feel truly seen, valued, and accompanied on their caregiving journey

## Content Structure

- **Introduction**
  - Begin by acknowledging what the reader may be feeling or facing
  - Reference relevant studies or personal anecdotes to set context
  - Include source article info when applicable

- **Main Content**
  - Move from acknowledging challenges to presenting solutions
  - Integrate research and data naturally, always connecting it to personal stories or scenarios
  - Be personal: if it feels right, share small, relatable anecdotes that illustrate points
  - Properly cite source material

- **Conclusion**
  - Offer clear, actionable steps readers can take
  - Suggest resources, including GiveCare, as practical next steps
  - End on a note of hope and encouragement, reinforcing that the reader is not alone

## Writing Style

- **Clarity First**
  - Keep explanations straightforward
  - Avoid unnecessary jargon
  - Make complex concepts accessible

- **Engaging Details**
  - Use metaphors or sensory details sparingly and only if they truly clarify a point
  - Help readers visualize scenarios in a relatable, friendly way

- **Inclusive Language**
  - Welcome all caregiving situations (parent, spouse, sibling, friend, globally)
  - Avoid cultural assumptions; consider diverse experiences

## Research Integration

- **Evidence-Based**
  - Use reputable, current sources
  - Highlight key findings clearly and explain why they matter
  - Always cite original sources

- **Human Connection**
  - Pair statistics or data with a short anecdote or scenario
  - Show how the data relates to everyday caregiving experiences
  - Make the research feel relevant, not distant

## Actionable Elements

- **Practical Tools**
  - Include specific tips, checklists, or mini-guides
  - Link to authoritative websites, hotlines, or local groups

- **Next Steps**
  - Suggest what to do next, such as exploring GiveCare’s SMS-based support
  - Make it easy for readers to implement suggestions right away

## Quality Checklist

Before finalizing your article, ensure:
- [ ] The tone is empathetic, conversational, and encouraging
- [ ] The structure is logical (intro → depth → advice → conclusion)
- [ ] Actionable takeaways are included
- [ ] Research is integrated smoothly and clearly
- [ ] The voice remains consistent and warm
- [ ] GiveCare is referenced in a way that feels natural, supportive, and aligned with the article’s theme
- [ ] Clear next steps or suggestions point readers toward helpful resources

By following these guidelines, you’ll produce content that not only informs caregivers but also uplifts and empowers them.
